Data Ingestion & Preprocessing 20 questions

Q1Your team ingests PDFs, HTML pages, and Confluence exports into a RAG system, but retrieval quality is poor for PDFs specifically. What's the likely root cause and fix?
Answer

PDFs often lose layout structure (tables, multi-column text, headers/footers) during naive text extraction, producing garbled or out-of-order text. Use a layout-aware parser (e.g. Unstructured, PyMuPDF with block detection, or a vision-based parser for scanned PDFs) and validate extraction quality with spot checks before chunking.

Q2A production RAG pipeline ingests nightly data dumps, but users report seeing stale answers for content updated hours ago. How do you fix the freshness gap?
Answer

Move from batch nightly ingestion to an event-driven or micro-batch pipeline (CDC from the source system, webhook-triggered re-indexing) so updates propagate within minutes, and add a "last updated" timestamp to indexed chunks so retrieval can be freshness-weighted.

Q3You're ingesting scanned legal contracts with no digital text layer. What approach do you take before these documents can enter the RAG pipeline?
Answer

Run OCR (Tesseract, AWS Textract, or a vision-language model) with layout preservation, then apply post-OCR cleanup (dehyphenation, whitespace normalization) and manual QA sampling, since OCR errors silently degrade retrieval and generation quality downstream.

Q4Your knowledge base includes duplicate documents uploaded under different filenames, and this is causing the same answer to appear multiple times in retrieved context. How do you address this at ingestion time?
Answer

Compute a content hash (or near-duplicate detection via MinHash/embedding similarity) at ingestion and deduplicate before indexing, keeping the most authoritative or most recent version and merging metadata.

Q21You switch from fixed 512-token chunking to semantic chunking and retrieval precision improves, but latency at ingestion time triples. Is this trade-off worth it in production, and how do you decide?
Answer

It depends on query volume versus ingestion frequency: semantic chunking (using embedding-based boundary detection or an LLM) is a one-time ingestion cost while retrieval quality benefits every query, so for high-QPS systems with infrequent re-ingestion it's usually worth it; mitigate cost with caching and async batch processing.

Q22Your chunks are 1000 tokens each, and answers about a specific clause in a 50-page contract keep missing because the clause spans two chunks. How do you fix this?
Answer

Add chunk overlap (e.g. 15-20% overlap between adjacent chunks) so boundary-spanning content appears fully in at least one chunk, and consider smaller chunks with a parent-document retrieval strategy that expands to surrounding context after a hit.

Answer

Embedding whole documents dilutes the embedding vector across unrelated topics, hurting retrieval precision, and exceeds practical context windows for ranking; chunking lets you retrieve the specific relevant passage and keep prompts focused, though you may still expand to full-document context after retrieval when needed.
